Common questions

Seattle sewer questions, answered

Is my side sewer my responsibility or the city's?

In Seattle, the side sewer — the pipe running from your home to the public main — is the property owner's responsibility for its entire length, including the portion under the street. That's why a failure there lands on you rather than the utility, and why an inspection before you buy a home is worth doing.

What does trenchless sewer repair cost?

It depends on the length, depth, diameter and condition of your line, and on how many branch connections have to be reinstated. We won't quote a number over the phone we can't stand behind — we scope the line on camera first, then give you an itemized price for both trenchless and traditional repair so you can compare. In most cases lining comes in below a full dig once restoration costs are included.

How long does a CIPP lining job take?

Most residential lining jobs are finished in a single day. The liner is inserted through an existing access point, cured in place, and the line is re-scoped on camera before we leave. Longer commercial runs or lines needing extensive root cutting can take two days.

How do I know if tree roots are in my sewer line?

The usual signs are gurgling toilets, several drains slowing at once, a lawn that's unusually green over the sewer path, or a line that backs up on roughly the same schedule every year. A camera inspection confirms it in about half an hour — and tells you whether cutting alone is enough or the pipe needs lining.

Do you offer emergency service?

Yes — we take calls 24 hours a day, 7 days a week at (425) 531-4847. If sewage is backing up into your home, call rather than submit the form so we can dispatch immediately.

What's covered by your warranty?

Our cured-in-place pipe lining work carries a 20-year warranty. We're also fully licensed, bonded and insured in Washington State (L&I #SEATTSS874JJ), with liability coverage on every job. Warranty terms are provided in writing with your completed work order.

Can you inspect a sewer line before I buy a house?

Absolutely, and in Seattle's older housing stock we'd strongly recommend it. A pre-purchase scope shows you the pipe material, any breaks or bellies, and root intrusion — information that's routinely worth thousands in negotiation or in problems avoided.
