The thing most keyword lists get wrong

Google reviews keywords, but keyword-level disapproval is rarely what bites a defense firm. The real exposure is match breadth: a phrase-match keyword like "criminal defense lawyer" will happily match criminal defense lawyer for sex charges, and now your ad has served against a sensitive query you never chose to bid on.

That is why the negatives below are not a housekeeping list. They are the control that keeps a compliant keyword from delivering a non-compliant impression — and on an account already carrying a policy flag, they go in before the keywords do.

Volume given up on purpose

Three of these negatives block searches with real volume that this firm could legitimately serve. That is the trade, and it is worth stating plainly rather than discovering later.

BlockedWhat it costsWhy block it anyway
marijuana cannabisA high-volume possession term in most markets.Names a controlled substance, which is the fastest route to a dangerous-products disapproval. Take it back only once the account is clean and unflagged.
trafficking distributionThe highest-value drug cases a firm sees.The classifier does not distinguish defending trafficking from facilitating it. These clients find firms by referral far more than by search.
gun firearm weaponWeapons charges are common alongside felony cases.Trips the dangerous-products filter regardless of framing. The felony keywords still catch much of this intent.

Note the one deliberate carve-out: sex offender is blocked as a phrase rather than offender as a word, so habitual offender searches — a real license-revocation status, and real business — stay biddable.

Running it

  • 1
    Negatives first, keywords secondUpload the shared lists and attach them to every campaign before a single keyword goes live. Reversing this order is how a flagged account gets flagged again.
  • 2
    Search Terms report weekly for the first monthThis is where you find out what you are actually matching. Anything sensitive that slips through becomes a new negative the same day.
  • 3
    Add negatives at the shared-list levelNot ad group level. A sensitive term you block in one place should be blocked everywhere, permanently.
  • 4
    Watch for "Rarely served" and "Low search volume"Different problems. Rarely served means Quality Score or policy limitation; low volume means the term is too narrow for the market and can be paused.
  • 5
    Re-run the conflict check after every editAdding one broad negative can silently disable an entire ad group, and nothing in the interface warns you.
