Verify before launch

Five claims are strong enough to be worth using and specific enough to be worth checking. Each one is either a policy exposure or a factual claim I could not confirm from directory data alone.

  • VERIFY
    Free Case ConsultationA Justia-syndicated directory lists "Free Consultation" beside the firm's number, but the site should say so plainly. If consultations are paid or case-dependent, cut this — it is the single most common source of legal-ad complaints.
  • VERIFY
    Direct Attorney AccessCredible for a three-person firm, but only if a caller actually reaches an attorney rather than an intake service.
  • VERIFY
    Walk-Ins WelcomeTaken from the Yelp business listing. Confirm it still holds — a stale walk-in promise wastes a click and irritates a prospect.
  • VERIFY
    Pender & Brunswick Co.Only run this if the firm actually appears in those courts and your geo-targeting reaches them. Otherwise keep the set inside New Hanover County.
  • VERIFY
    Top 100 Trial LawyersThe National Trial Lawyers designation is on Allard's directory profile. Confirm the year is current — NC Rule 7.1 treats a lapsed honor as a misleading claim.

Do not run these

Each of these is a callout a criminal-defense account tends to attract, and each one is either a Google policy problem, a North Carolina Rules of Professional Conduct problem, or simply the wrong practice area.

  • NO
    No Fee Unless We WinContingency language. It belongs to personal injury and is meaningless in criminal defense — this is the mistake to avoid if a PI template gets reused here.
  • NO
    Best Defense AttorneyUnverifiable superlative. NC Rule 7.1 bars comparative claims that can't be factually substantiated, and Google flags them too.
  • NO
    Board Certified in DWINC Rule 7.4 permits a specialist claim only with NC State Bar Board of Legal Specialization certification. Nothing in the record shows it.
  • NO
    Charges DroppedA past-results claim. NC Rule 7.1 requires a disclaimer that outcomes depend on the facts, and a 25-character callout has no room for one.
  • NO
    Call (910) 763-0156Google disallows phone numbers and calls-to-action in callouts. Put the number in a call asset instead.
  • NO
    Available 24/7Posted hours are Mon–Thu 9–5 and Fri 9–3. Only run an availability callout if there is a genuine after-hours intake line.

How to load them

Put the ten Core callouts at the account level so every campaign inherits a floor. Then attach each practice group at the ad group level, where the callout matches the keyword intent — someone searching "DWI lawyer Wilmington" should see DWI callouts, not drug-charge ones. Google serves up to ten and picks per auction, so 6–10 relevant assets per ad group beats loading all twenty. Callouts do not click, so keep them as proof rather than instruction: no "Call Today", no offers, no punctuation tricks. Title Case throughout, which is what these are already set in.
